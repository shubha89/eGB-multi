**The earlier one-year/50 s diagnostic did not match Figure 8.** That setup
belongs to the separate wide mismatch grid described in the paper.
Our original full-year run has **1,540.898 times more retained samples** than
the Figure 8 run. The additional sample work, different TDI settings, cached
geometry, and unequal JAXGB output/timing boundaries explain why the previous
native-workload ratios cannot be compared to Figure 8's ratios. Sample-count
scaling is a workload explanation, not an exact performance scaling law.

The eGB timer includes both links and pyTDI, and excludes geometry, A/E/T
conversion, calibration, and mismatch evaluation. The JAXGB timer includes
parameter packing, native response generation, NumPy materialization,
zero-padding, inverse FFTs, and cropping to 4,096 samples. Consequently,
the missing explicit `block_until_ready()` in this script is not evidence of
an asynchronous timing error: the timed paths materialize NumPy arrays.

**Resolution caveat:** the Figure 8 grid has Nyquist frequency 3.386 mHz.
At a 3 mHz carrier, higher eccentric harmonics can exceed that limit. These
short-grid timings must not be presented as an accuracy-validated full-year
benchmark; cadence convergence is a separate requirement.

Suggested clarification for the Figure 8 caption: "The timing benchmark uses
a seven-day observation with 4,096 samples (147.65625 s cadence), precomputed
geometry, pyTDI generation 1 with order-5 interpolation, and JAXGBaccurate with
128 slow-response samples and time-domain reconstruction. The shading spans
eccentricity cases, not repeated timings. This benchmark is separate from the
one-year mismatch grid."

## What is stated in the paper

[Figure 8, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24546#page=13) plots eGB-multi
medians of approximately 16–18 ms and JAXGBaccurate times of approximately
4–8 ms; these are visual readings, not machine-readable table values.
The text reports a median relative cost of about 2.7.

Section IV specifies a one-year, 50 s, 631,152-sample **wide mismatch grid**.
The text describes precomputed geometry. Figure 8 does not separately identify
its timed sample count, slow-response resolution, warmup/JIT policy, or exact
timer boundaries. It is therefore not established that its timings use the
entire wide mismatch grid's observation length and cadence.

Its comparator is JAXGBaccurate, and its hardware is an Apple M4 with 16 GB
memory. Figure 8 covers 0.1, 0.3, 1, and 3 mHz, with 0.5 + 0.5 solar masses.

## Differences in our first benchmark

- Basic `JaxGB`, explicitly JIT-compiled, with 2,048 Fourier bins; its sparse
  output is not reconstructed to a dense time series.
- eGB produces a complete year at 5 s cadence: ten times the wide-grid sample
  count stated in the paper. It computes cached-shape blocks with halos,
  timing geometry, links, and pyTDI separately.
- Geometry is rebuilt and included for eGB, but cached and excluded for JAXGB.
- Our frequency grid is 1, 3, 10 mHz; masses are 0.6 + 0.4 solar masses.
- Both sides synchronize completed numerical outputs; setup and compilation
  are outside warm execution times. No matching timing protocol for Figure 8
  has been recovered.

## Measured diagnostic

`benchmarks/paper_comparison.py` uses the installed checkout and libraries,
one year at 50 s cadence, one contiguous array, cached geometry, one source
at 1 mHz and e=0.3, fixed 1PN physics, and generation-2 pyTDI XYZ. It retains
our source parameters and response implementation; it is a diagnostic rather
than a claimed reproduction of Figure 8.

| Measured warm quantity | Median |
|---|---:|
| eGB links + pyTDI, cached geometry | 1.417 s |
| eGB links alone | 0.176 s |
| pyTDI alone | 1.247 s |
| JAXGBaccurate, 2,048 points, no outer JIT | 2.983 ms |
| JAXGBaccurate, 4,096 points, no outer JIT | 3.263 ms |
| JAXGBaccurate, 2,048 points, outer JIT | 0.355 ms |
| JAXGBaccurate, 4,096 points, outer JIT | 0.641 ms |

Component medians need not sum to the median total. eGB has three warm repeats;
JAXGBaccurate has ten per configuration. JAXGBaccurate entries retain native
frequency-domain output. No equal-accuracy claim is made.

Changing cadence and caching reduces our eGB time from roughly 17 s to 1.42 s,
but a residual discrepancy of about 80-fold remains relative to the plotted
approximately 18 ms value near 1 mHz. Even the isolated full-year link time
exceeds the plotted eGB total.

The remaining discrepancy cannot be attributed confidently to hardware or
cadence. Resolving it requires the Figure 8 benchmark script and exact code/
dependency versions, particularly the actual timed sample count, inclusion of
pyTDI, cache policy, and synchronization/compilation policy. A shorter timed
segment, different timed stages, or another implementation could account for
the difference, but none is established from the paper alone.

See [raw diagnostic timings](paper_diagnostic.json). The original timing data
are preserved. Their ratios should not be cited as a reproduction or refutation
of Figure 8.
